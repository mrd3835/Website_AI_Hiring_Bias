The Illusion of Neutrality: Bias in AI Recruitment Systems

What are we teaching our children, and more importantly, what are we teaching our software?

The Challenge of AI Hiring

Hiring employees can be a long process, from getting the word out that a company is hiring, to reading hundreds of resumes to find the most qualified applicants. Many companies have found this process to be expensive and time consuming, so they invested in algorithms that could do it for them. However, AI-driven hiring isn’t free from problems itself. While some argue that these tools save companies money and reduce human prejudice, the evidence suggests that they often replicate existing bias. AI hiring practices use selective job advertisements, biased data in machine learning, and proxies for characteristics to disadvantage minority groups in the hiring process, and there needs to be laws against such algorithms to limit the discrimination in the hiring process.

Key Takeaways

Hiring algorithms use selective job advertisements, biased data in machine learning, and proxies for characteristics to replicate biased hiring done by humans, and by extension disadvantage minority groups significantly. A hiring algorithm's whole goal is to replicate how humans hire people, so the decisions that algorithms make truly expose the biases in hiring practice. One can't stop an algorithm replicating biased humans from being biased, so we need to prohibit the use of hiring algorithms in order to protect minority groups from further discrimination in the hiring process. Before we can make an algorithm that is not biased, we have to not be biased ourselves. Future research could examine whether transparency requirements, third-party audits, or carefully designed data collection methods might allow AI hiring tools to be used more fairly. Until such safeguards exist, the risks of discrimination outweigh the benefits of efficiency.
