Resume Review and Algorithmic Bias

Even well-qualified applicants can be filtered out by biased algorithms.

Resume Review

Even if someone in a minority group hears about a job, they are disadvantaged in the next step of the hiring process as well. Many companies use hiring algorithms to help save time and minimize discrimination in hiring practices. Supporters of AI hiring argue that these programs can reduce the influence of human prejudice and make resume screening more efficient. Yet, the very data that trains the AI is infused with past discrimination, meaning the systems reproduce the same inequities they are supposed to solve.

If the underlying performance data is polluted by lingering effects of sexism, racism, or other forms of structural bias, de-biasing a hiring algorithm built from that data is merely a band-aid on a festering wound.

—Miranda Bogen, Harvard Business Review (2019)

The Goal

Companies originally thought that using machine learning to make predictions based on past screening decisions was saving employers time and, purportedly, minimizing the effect of human prejudice. While machine learning is saving time and money for employers, it is actually recreating the bias in today's hiring practices because they used machine learning. Machine learning is when large amounts of data are entered into a computer so that it learns how to complete a task. It is very hard for a computer to understand what makes a good candidate when hiring, so researchers give it examples of how people hire so it can try to copy those decisions. This process is how most researchers make hiring algorithms. The goal was that an algorithm wouldn’t be able to participate in human prejudices and be able to hire applicants more fairly.

The Outcome

However, the data entered into the computer was unknowingly full of prejudice. The companies fed the machines their biased data and accidentally taught the computer to be biased as well. Because the data entered into hiring algorithms is polluted with bias, minority groups are disadvantaged regardless of if they are being hired by a person or a computer. Because the ‘fair’ computer algorithm was built using biased hiring data in the first place, it is unable to complete the task it was designed for. Researchers are trying to make computers less biased, but their work is proven useless because they are biased themselves. The algorithm will continue to hire the applicants according to major companies’ past hiring data, which means hiring women or people of color disproportionately to the number of qualified applications. In order to stop this trend, hiring algorithms that introduce bias must be prohibited.
