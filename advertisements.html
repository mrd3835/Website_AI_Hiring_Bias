Algorithmic Job Advertisements

Targeted ads shape career paths before applications are ever submitted.

Job Advertisements

Job advertisements control what demographics have access to certain jobs and contribute to the disadvantage that minority groups face in the hiring process. Because job advertisements are distributed by an algorithm toward the demographics who are most likely to click on them, Miranda Bogen, a Policy Manager who specializes in AI fairness, found that Facebook’s advertising algorithm sent different job advertisements to people based on their race or gender in a recent study.

The machine learned that more people who are black would click on a job ad for taxi companies, so it sent the ad to an audience that was approximately 75% black.

—Miranda Bogen, Harvard Business Review (2019)

The Problem

This AI advertisement controlled the access that black people had to jobs where they were the minority in that field. However, race isn’t the only factor that is affected by selective advertising. Karen Hao, the senior AI editor at MIT Technology Review, found that the jobs advertisements also differed based on the user’s gender. The advertisements for “software engineers for Nvidia [were] skewed male and Netflix [were] skewed female, and sales associates for cars [were] skewed male and jewelry [were] skewed female.”

The Effect

This perpetuates the difference in hiring practices, because as more people from a community apply to a particular job, more people of that community will be hired. For example, more women were shown ads for job applications for jewelry, which means that women are going to be the primary people who apply and get hired by the jewelry company. Even though anyone could have applied for the job, only certain demographics could know about the job because of this AI bias. While jobs should be available to everyone, selective advertising through computer algorithms continues to sort users and disadvantage some communities. Algorithms that advertise jobs based on demographics control what demographics have access to certain jobs and contribute substantially to the disadvantage that minority groups face in the hiring process, so there needs to be laws prohibiting hiring algorithms that contribute to this disadvantage.
